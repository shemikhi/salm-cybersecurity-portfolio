The SOC Analyst Mindset: Alert Triage Like a Hunter

2025-07-20 · 7 min read · Salm Bashemakh

Beyond Checkbox Triage

Most SOC analysts treat alerts as tickets to close. The hunter mindset treats every alert as a potential thread in a larger story. Here's how I approach alert triage differently.

The Hunter's Framework

1. Context First

Before touching the alert, I ask: What is this asset? Who uses it? What's its criticality? A PowerShell execution on a developer's workstation is different from the same on a Domain Controller.

2. Pattern Recognition

Single alerts are noise. Patterns are signal. I always look for:

  • Temporal clustering (multiple alerts in short time windows)
  • Asset clustering (same technique across multiple hosts)
  • Technique chaining (recon → initial access → lateral movement)

3. Hypothesis-Driven Investigation

Instead of asking "Is this alert true or false?", ask "If an attacker were here, what would they do next?" Then hunt for those behaviors.

Reducing False Positives

# Example: Filter out known-good PowerShell usage
index=sysmon EventCode=1
| search CommandLine="*powershell*"
| lookup known_good_scripts.csv script_hash OUTPUT description
| where isnull(description)
| stats count by Computer, User, CommandLine
| where count < 5

Building Intuition

Intuition comes from repetition and failure. Every false positive teaches you something about your environment. Document everything. Build your own mental model of "normal."

"The best SOC analysts don't just respond to alerts — they anticipate attacks before the alert fires."
